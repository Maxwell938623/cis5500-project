1. Motivation and Problem Description
The rapidly increasing prominence of fare evasion in New York City subways has become a hot political and economic topic. According to the Citizens Budget Commission, “Fare and toll evasion cost the Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA) approximately $1 billion in revenue in 2024”. Currently, the MTA has an “approximately $800 million structural operating budget gap,” and rates of crime, arrests, and graffiti/public property vandalization have also subsequently increased. Thus, working to reduce fare evasion on the MTA is an important project to secure the future of New York City and its millions of inhabitants. Thus, we hope to create a website that aims to inform future city-planning and security measures surrounding public transportation in New York City. Our intended audience includes law enforcement, the MTA itself, and other security. 


2. List of Major Features (Definitely Implement)
We hope to include the following interactive pages on our website:
1. Borough-Level Equity Comparison
   1. A comparative visualization dashboard (using bar charts or radar plots) that aggregates data by NYC borough (Brooklyn, Manhattan, Queens, Bronx). It will display the "Enforcement-to-Rider" ratio, normalized by 100,000 riders, to highlight which boroughs experience the highest intensity of police intervention relative to their actual subway traffic. 
2. Financial Impact Analysis
   1. A dashboard displaying fare evasion-related analytics. For example, estimated lost revenue, estimated fare-evaders per day of the weak/season/year, etc. A user can input hypothetical changes to the MTA fare and use historical fare evasion with respect to fare price to estimate change in revenue.
3. Station Safety Index
   1. A dropdown menu with search capabilities where users can query specific stations in the MTA network. After selecting a station, the page will show that particular station’s attributes, such as its rank in NYC enforcement actions and ratio of arrests to summons. Users can also filter by regions, transit lines, and time periods to see enforcement rates at different locations and time periods. 


3. List of Features (Might Implement)
1. A Geospatial Heatmap of Law Enforcement vs. Traffic
   1. Interactive map of NYC embedding a graph of subway station nodes. The heatmap will have two layers: “Ridership Volume” (derived from turnstile data) and “Law Enforcement Presence” (derived from NYPD arrests/reports).
2. Historical Time-Series Trend Analysis
   1. A multi-line chart tracking longitudinal data from 2018 to 2024. Users can toggle between "Estimated Evasion Rates" (Dataset 2) and "Total NYPD Arrests/Summons" (Dataset 3). The graph will allow users to filter by "Season" to see if enforcement spikes during colder months or holiday periods.
